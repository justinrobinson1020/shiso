## Design Context

### Users
One person: the owner, checking personal finances on a desktop browser and a phone. Sessions are short and task-driven: see what is due and what cash is left this month, categorize new transactions, adjust envelope assignments, glance at spending trends. The app replaces a Google Sheet, so density and scannability matter more than presentation.

### Brand Personality
Quiet, precise, dependable. The interface should feel like a well-kept ledger: nothing shouts, numbers line up, state is obvious at a glance. No marketing tone, no celebration moments.

### Aesthetic Direction
Spreadsheet-descended density on warm-tinted neutrals (off-white light theme, near-black dark theme) with a single green accent and red for negatives. Light and dark both follow the OS setting. Keep the palette; refine typography beyond system-ui and reduce card-wrapping so hierarchy comes from spacing and rules rather than boxes. Anti-references: neon fintech dashboards, glowing charts, hero metrics, gradient text, glassmorphism.

### Accessibility
No formal requirement. Personal single-user app; the owner has stated accessibility is not a goal. Do not spend effort on WCAG conformance beyond what falls out of good defaults.

### Design Principles
1. Numbers first: tabular figures, right-aligned money, sign and color agree.
2. Density over decoration: tables and rules before cards and shadows.
3. One accent, used for the primary action and the active state only.
4. Same interface on phone, adapted not amputated: hidden columns must reappear as sublines or inputs.
5. State is visible without hover: paid, pending, overdue, drift, and review counts read in one glance.
